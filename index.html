Foveation enables efficient perceptive RL

Rendering and encoding dense images consume resources that could support more parallel learning. Foveation concentrates detail around gaze in a compact image while retaining peripheral context.

Rendering and policy architecture

Render where the robot looks

We use OmniLensDistortionLutAPI to attach forward and inverse lookup textures to the RTX camera. RTX traces rays directly into a compact foveated image. Gaze moves the high-detail center while retaining the wider scene. On the robot, software samples the same view from a fixed fisheye camera.

Learn from vision, language, and touch

A frozen, foveation-adapted visual encoder feeds a recurrent policy alongside the instruction, tactile feedback, and robot state. Reinforcement learning trains the motor policy, with supervised gaze learning and a privileged critic used only during training.

Reverse lookup: direction to pixel

Tracing starts with a pixel and asks where it looks. Operations such as motion-vector computation ask the reverse: where does this direction land in the image?

We analytically invert the angular mapping and CMF warp, then sample that inverse into a second texture. Its square domain is an octahedral encoding of directions over the whole sphere. Its two stored values are the corresponding forward-texture coordinates.
